# Phase 6D — employee invitation administration

This phase adds the Owner/Admin invitation history, detail, original email-request retry, and durable revocation screens. The existing Supabase Auth invitation and magic-link delivery paths remain the credential boundary. Local validation is recorded in [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md); deployment and actual email/callback acceptance remain separate checks described in [INVITATIONS.md](INVITATIONS.md).

## Current authority and historical scope

The new RPCs require the current verified, active Owner/Admin membership and the configured privileged MFA assurance. A scoped Admin must retain assignments to every branch in the invitation's original branch set. Those historical assignments are checked directly, including inactive branches; access to only the first branch does not expose the remaining invitation or its audit. An all-branch administrator may read the organization history. Only an Owner may revoke an invitation whose requested role is Admin. Displayed capabilities are hints, and each action repeats current authorization in PostgreSQL.

Historical visibility does not revive creation or delivery authority. The existing preparation, delivery-claim, and acceptance helpers still require eligible active branches and the inviter's current role and assignments. A caller must retain current business access to enter administration; a historical branch alone does not create a session or business membership. Preview is empty and read only.

The managed list supports literal, case-insensitive search over email, name, and employee ID, plus pending/accepted/revoked/expired filters. SQL bounds offsets to 0–100,000 and result limits to 1–100. The repository accepts pages of at most 99 so one extra row can establish continuation; the screen displays pages of 20. Ordering is creation time descending, then invitation ID. Historical branch names and active flags are current branch metadata; the stored assignment IDs remain the original invitation scope. Server timestamps are explicit UTC.

## Business status and email-request evidence

Business status and delivery state are separate. Accepted or revoked timestamps determine terminal business status before expiry; an otherwise eligible record becomes expired when its business invitation expiry passes. Pending means awaiting employee acceptance and does not grant access. The detail keeps the original role, assignments, contact information, timestamps, and recorded delivery state visible to authorized reviewers.

`sent` means Supabase Auth accepted the email request and the SQL delivery receipt was recorded. It does not establish inbox arrival, a read receipt, or employee acceptance. `sending` means dispatch is in flight or its outcome is unknown. Provider timeouts, HTTP 5xx responses, and a lost delivery-receipt write may leave this state even when Auth accepted the email. It has no timed reclaim, automatic resend, forced-failure reset, or replacement-invitation action. `failed` records a known rejected Auth request; `prepared` means the business invitation exists before a recorded delivery claim.

Migration 5 added `delivery_state` with a `prepared` default to older records. An already accepted legacy invitation can therefore retain `prepared` and lack delivery timestamps. Administration preserves that history without inventing a `sent` fact or rejecting the whole page. Accepted legacy records have no delivery retry and cannot be revoked to administer their resulting membership. A legacy record without the complete original request payload cannot retry delivery; missing original branch assignments also leave it read only. Eligible pending records with retained branch scope can still use the current authorized revocation workflow.

Revocation and email state can coexist. An email already claimed may still arrive after the invitation is revoked, and its final Auth acceptance can still be recorded as `sent`. The UI presents revoked business status alongside that retained evidence. It does not claim to recall an email.

## Retry the exact original email request

Only the original inviter can receive a retry capability. The record must remain pending and unexpired, have delivery state `prepared` or `failed`, retain a complete original payload, and satisfy current invitation role/active-branch authority. The detail returns `retry_input` only in that case. Another authorized administrator may inspect ordinary invitation metadata but does not receive the creator's original retry payload.

The retry payload contains only the original organization ID, request UUID, normalized email, full name, requested role, branch IDs, all-branch flag, employee ID, phone, and reason. SQL verifies it matches the stored original payload; an arbitrary stored JSON key, reconstructed reason, changed phone, or replacement UUID cannot substitute for the original action. Delivery claims, Auth credentials, token URLs, and opaque request payloads are not exposed. Phone remains ordinary authorized contact metadata; the exact original reason and request UUID are supplied through the restricted retry payload.

Before invoking the Edge Function, the repository reloads current detail and compares the exact original input. It passes that same request UUID and fields through the existing actor-context invitation endpoint, then checks the returned invitation ID and expiry against the original. The Edge Function validates the bearer user against Auth, preparation rechecks current SQL authority, and the service-only claim rechecks the inviter before dispatch. `sending` and `sent` requests never dispatch another email. Known failed delivery has the existing ten-second retry cooldown. New invitations retain the organization-wide rolling limits of 20 per ten minutes and 200 per 24 hours; known original-request replay remains available at those creation limits. Supabase Auth's own email limits and production SMTP remain separate deployment requirements.

There is no administrative reconciliation that guesses the outcome of unknown sending, sends a replacement email, changes expiry, or changes the original role/assignments. Acceptance continues to require the recipient's current verified matching email, required password setup, sent delivery evidence for the current lifecycle, unexpired/unrevoked status, and current inviter/branch authority. A business invitation UUID is a lookup identifier, never an acceptance credential.

## Durable revocation and concurrency

The application calls `revoke_employee_invitation_action` with the invitation ID, original expected revision, meaningful reason, and one stable request UUID. The existing native encrypted operation journal saves intent before the RPC and records each attempt before dispatch. New invitation revocations and delivery retries are blocked while a saved revocation remains pending. Retry uses the original actor, organization, UUID, revision, reason, and payload; a journal's branch locator may become inactive and is not authorization.

SQL checks current verified membership, MFA, target-role hierarchy, and the complete historical branch assignment before returning even an immutable successful replay. The request UUID binds one exact normalized payload. For a new action, SQL locks the invitation, checks its revision and pending state, and atomically writes the revoked timestamp, immutable action receipt, request receipt, and audit. A failed audit rolls the whole action back. The revision trigger covers updates from the older prepare/claim/finish/accept/revoke paths as well as the new action, so an older detail cannot overwrite a newer lifecycle state. No stock, money, employee membership, or offline grant is changed by invitation revocation.

Only a first-attempt definitive SQL rollback can mark the local request rejected. Missing, malformed, or incorrectly scoped success evidence remains pending. A later permission or validation denial after an uncertain attempt does not prove that the earlier action failed, so the saved request remains unresolved. Successful decoding checks the original request, actor, organization, invitation, and resulting revision before local confirmation. Signout, recovery, account replacement, and app lock are checked around asynchronous access; pending evidence remains with its original account.

Acceptance, revocation, delivery claim, and delivery finish serialize on the invitation row. Acceptance and revocation cannot both win the same pending invitation. Revocation committed before a claim prevents dispatch; revocation after a claim prevents business acceptance while preserving the unknown or final mail evidence. Concurrent original revocation transports recover one immutable receipt and audit. Concurrent claim transports issue one delivery claim, and no other transport takes over its unknown outcome.

The new history and detail RPCs are `list_managed_employee_invitations` and `get_managed_employee_invitation`. Their definer helpers and immutable action table are ungranted to application roles. The audit policy is rebound to the wrapper that checks the full immutable event branch snapshot, including inactive branches; earlier non-invitation audit rules remain in force. Existing recipient and delivery APIs remain separate from administrator history.

## UI and validation boundary

The screen clears retained private data on repository, role, or preview-context replacement. Dialog leases hide the old detail and disable its actions when that source changes; asynchronous reads and mutations carry the original repository and page generation. Production repositories also reject recovery and locked contexts. The existing whole-Navigator session lock covers retained dialogs. Reloads use recorded server state, and notices distinguish an accepted email request from an unresolved one.

Required local checks cover complete and partial historical scope, current MFA and role changes, creator-only payload access, exact original retry fields, immutable request replay, changed payload/CAS denial, older lifecycle revision changes, audit rollback, acceptance/revocation and claim races, encrypted journal restart, malformed confirmations, later denial after uncertainty, stale dialogs/responses, literal search/paging, and narrow layouts. Deterministic SQL, function, repository, and widget fixtures do not send email or establish live provider, callback, operating-system, or physical-device acceptance. No custom email provider, arbitrary Auth-user inspection, or client service credential is introduced.
