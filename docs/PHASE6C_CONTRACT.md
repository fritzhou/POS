# Phase 6C — receipt copies and keyboard scanners

Status: implemented in the working tree. Local validation and remaining release gates are recorded in [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md). This phase adds receipt output and barcode input to existing online and offline workflows. It does not add payment authorization, new financial mutations, camera scanning, or direct printer protocols.

## Original receipt evidence

`ReceiptDocument` is an immutable presentation snapshot built from an existing committed `SaleReceipt` or preserved `LocalOfflineReceipt`. It validates supported currency, exact integer amounts, bounded unique items and payments, line arithmetic, aggregate totals, payment allocations, reversal limits, and offline link metadata before customer output is available. Output failure leaves the original record available for review.

Online copies use the receipt's historical business, branch, address, contact, footer, product names, SKUs, sale prices, quantities, discounts, tax, and payment evidence. They do not fetch current settings or catalog values to replace that history. Original totals and allocations remain visible after refunds or voids; cumulative reversed quantities and amounts appear separately. The receipt snapshot does not establish the type or timestamp of individual reversals, so a copy does not invent a refund date or a void receipt.

Every output identifies itself as a receipt copy. PDF page headers and footers repeat `Receipt copy`, and native output filenames end in `_COPY.pdf`. Output is an explicit operator action; completing a sale does not automatically print or share it. Receipt number text is not used as a native path: the service derives a bounded filename from the receipt origin and a hash of organization, branch, and receipt identifiers.

An offline customer copy always remains `PROVISIONAL OFFLINE RECEIPT`, including after a verified synchronization link is recorded. It preserves the original offline receipt number, event identifier, local sale identifier, sequence, observed time, and synchronization/review status. A linked server receipt and any retained acceptance time appear separately; they do not replace the original local identity or turn the customer copy into an authoritative server receipt. Failed and reconciliation-required records retain their original paid evidence and instruct the reviewer not to collect another payment.

Cash received and change appear only when the source retains the original tendered amount. The offline sale model retains allocated cash but no cash tender/change snapshot, so customer output omits those values. Offline output also does not invent a tax rate, address, contact, footer, unit conversion, or other absent historical field. Noncash payments retain the operator's manual reference and explicitly state that provider authorization is not claimed. Source times use Asia/Manila in the receipt view. The customer PDF preserves the full source instant when displaying `Server recorded`, `Device observed`, and separately linked `Server accepted` times in Asia/Manila with an explicit UTC+08:00 label; the presentation model retains the underlying UTC timestamps.

Customer PDFs exclude signed offline authorization, grant material, private keys, cached product/stock snapshots, supplier costs, and other reviewer proof. The existing `Copy original signed record` action remains a separate authorized-review export through `OfflineWorkspace.exportReceipt`; it is not the customer PDF action. Its clipboard handoff checks the same current output scope after its asynchronous read.

## PDF generation and platform adapters

The renderer uses bundled regular/bold Noto Sans and a bundled CJK fallback. It does not download fonts, fetch business information, or depend on a network connection to render a receipt. It checks source text against the bundled font coverage before rendering. Supported text is limited to covered characters in Unicode's Basic Multilingual Plane. Astral characters above U+FFFF are rejected even when a font contains a glyph: the pinned `pdf` package's ToUnicode encoding cannot correctly preserve those characters as UTF-16 surrogate pairs. This avoids a visually plausible PDF with corrupted searchable or extractable text.

Unsupported characters cause export to fail rather than silently substitute missing glyphs. The service returns a distinct `unsupportedCharacters` result, and the UI displays a clear notice and disables output for that frozen view instead of urging the operator to repeat an impossible export; original history remains readable and unchanged. Bundled Unicode coverage is finite and is not a promise of every script, emoji, or shaping requirement.

Paper choices are A4, 80 mm, and 58 mm. Thermal documents use finite 200 mm page heights and pagination, with a repeated receipt-copy header and page-number footer. They are multipage PDFs rather than an unlimited roll or a single clipped bitmap. The native print request uses the selected format with dynamic layout disabled. Actual paper selection, margins, scaling, driver behavior, and printer completion belong to the operating system and printer. Output preparation is bounded; invalid PDF bytes or an oversized result fail before handoff.

`NativeReceiptOutputService` renders bytes, verifies the current output guard, and delegates through `ReceiptOutputGateway`. The production gateway uses the pinned `printing` package's `Printing.layoutPdf` and `Printing.sharePdf` implementations. Injected gateways and renderers provide test seams without acknowledging payments or manufacturing receipt acceptance.

On Windows, the package's `sharePdf` implementation writes the customer PDF into the system temporary directory and opens it with the registered PDF handler through `ShellExecuteEx`. It is an open-PDF handoff, not a Windows share-picker or a guaranteed Save As dialog. Saving or sharing uses that handler's controls. The adapter does not establish that a handler is installed, that a user saved the document, or that temporary output is removed on logout.

On Android, the package writes the customer PDF into its share cache and launches an OS share chooser with a FileProvider URI and file-access permission. The pinned adapter uses a read-permission intent and also explicitly grants read/write URI permission to resolved chooser activities; the customer export is not a signed or tamper-proof record. Print uses the Android system print service. A compatible installed handler or print service is required; neither an OS handoff nor its returned boolean proves delivery to a recipient or a physical print. The app's notices describe submission/opening, cancellation, unavailability, or failure, without claiming completed physical output.

Once an explicitly requested PDF is handed to an external handler, share recipient, print service, or spooler, that external copy is outside the Flutter session lock and encrypted local database. The app does not claim to revoke or erase those customer copies. This phase implements system PDF printing only: no ESC/POS, Bluetooth/USB printer pairing, cash-drawer command, direct thermal-printer transport, or physical-device certification is included.

## Scope and access boundaries

`ReceiptOutputScope` covers the Navigator under `SessionLockGuard`. A receipt view captures its output authorization epoch, access-lock instance/generation, current actor/business context, and allowed state. The production scope requires the current verified identity to match the selected business actor, excludes preview/recovery/known-denied access, and tracks membership/security revisions and offline workspace changes. Output without a scope or an explicit current-source guard fails closed.

Call sites also retain their original repository/workspace, branch, and page generation. Sales history invalidates its retained receipt view when its source changes. Output actions require the original view to remain mounted and the route to remain current, freeze the selected document/service for each attempt, and check again after asynchronous rendering before native handoff. The system print gateway checks the guard immediately before invoking `Printing.layoutPdf` and copies the already rendered bytes into a private frozen snapshot. Closing a view, source replacement, signout/recovery, access denial, or a lock-generation change prevents a pending attempt from reaching that handoff. Unlocking does not revive a pre-handoff attempt prepared under the earlier generation; reopen the receipt to capture current authority.

The explicit native invocation is the output authorization boundary. Android's print UI can pause Flutter and engage the ordinary app lock before asking for PDF layout. Later native layout callbacks return fresh copies of only the already authorized frozen PDF; they do not consult a replacement account, read another source, or render a new document. Subsequent lock/signout does not revoke that external job, just as it cannot retract a PDF already shared. Normal lifecycle locking remains enabled, and every new output action still requires current app authority. The callback boundary permits completion of the selected customer-copy job without creating a session-lock exemption or authorizing further app disclosure.

The whole-Navigator lock hides retained receipt dialogs from view, focus, and semantics while locked. Current-source guards protect pending output and clipboard handoffs; they do not grant server authority or replace existing membership/MFA checks. Existing authorized read paths supply the receipt snapshots. Expired or released offline authority does not manufacture a new sale, and the output layer does not require a new capture merely to represent already preserved evidence.

Receipt rendering/output code performs no checkout, reversal, expense, purchase, stock, synchronization, journal-confirmation, or other financial RPC. Print/share success or failure does not change original sale totals, tender evidence, synchronization acceptance, request UUIDs, or encrypted pending operations.

## Keyboard HID scanning

Online POS and the separate offline workspace offer an opt-in `Keyboard scanner` switch and configuration dialog. The scanner starts off. Default framing is F9, the barcode burst, then Enter; a numeric-keypad Enter is also accepted. Configuration can select no prefix for rapid bursts, Enter or Tab termination, and a maximum inter-key gap of 25, 40, or 70 milliseconds. Configuration is local to the mounted capture widget; it is not a persistent device-pairing or business setting.

The decoder accepts exactly 4–128 ASCII characters in the range `!` through `~`, with no spaces, within a two-second burst and the configured timing gap. It preserves case and leading zeroes. Non-ASCII characters, control characters, key repeats, Ctrl/Alt/Meta combinations, malformed framing, backwards timestamps, excessive length, and slow bursts are rejected. A rejected framed burst cannot become an accepted valid-looking suffix; rejection lasts until a terminator or fresh F9 prefix. Physical Shift transitions used for uppercase characters are ignored as modifiers.

No-prefix rapid mode cannot reliably distinguish a scanner from sufficiently fast human typing; the UI recommends F9 for separation. Capture is confined to its focused, current POS route while the app is in the foreground, the switch is enabled, no editable field is focused, and ordering/capture is currently allowed. Dialogs, forms, unavailable checkout state, and inactive routes pause capture. Losing focus or availability resets a partial burst; backgrounding disables capture, and source/scope replacement disables and invalidates the previous capture.

A complete burst performs the existing exact barcode lookup in the selected branch. The asynchronous lookup carries a current-scope predicate and checks it before changing the cart. Online lookup remains server-backed; offline lookup remains confined to the authorized prepared cache and its existing capture limits. Scanning adds an eligible existing product to the cart; it does not collect money, bypass payment confirmation, or submit checkout.

The same barcode repeated less than 750 milliseconds after the preceding dispatched burst is suppressed. Additional bursts during lookup are discarded rather than queued. These controls prevent rapid accidental duplicate additions; a deliberate later scan can add another unit subject to the ordinary cart and stock checks.

USB and Bluetooth devices are supported only when the operating system exposes them as keyboard HID input with compatible framing. This code does not discover, pair, authenticate, or communicate directly with a scanner. Camera scanning remains `Coming Soon`; no camera capture/decoder or new camera permission is implemented. Synthetic key-event and rendering checks do not certify a physical scanner, printer, Windows installation, Android print service, or live Supabase deployment.
